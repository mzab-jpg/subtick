Expanding the Radius of Daily Life

BY PACKY McCORMICK & TSUNG XU · JUN 8

The history of human civilization is a story of expanding the radius of our daily lives. Before the wheel, our world was limited to what we could walk to in a day. The horse expanded that radius, and trains and automobiles shattered it completely, fundamentally reshaping where and how we live. The suburbs, the modern city layout, the commute—all are products of the automobile's particular speed and infrastructure requirements.

Now, we are on the precipice of another fundamental shift. Electric Vertical Takeoff and Landing (eVTOL) vehicles—flying cars—promise to move us in a straight line, untethered from the rigid grid of asphalt and concrete. They aren't just a faster way to get from A to B; they are a new geometry for daily living.

"The car didn't just move us — it rearranged the world around us."

Imagine living in a quiet mountain town but working in a bustling metropolis, the commute taking minutes instead of hours. The implications for real estate, urban planning, and economic opportunity are staggering. We are not just inventing a new vehicle; we are inventing a new kind of map. The radius of daily life is about to expand once again.
